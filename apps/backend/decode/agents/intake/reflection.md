against the findings you recorded.

Check in this order:

1. **Grounding.** Does every claim about the material trace to something you recorded? Where a
   statement rests on your own knowledge rather than on the source, either record the finding that
   supports it or move it to open_questions. Pedagogy is exempt: analogies, examples and framings
   are your craft, not claims — do not record findings for them, and do not cut them for lacking
   one.
2. **Scope against runtime.** Read scope_in as a video running at the target duration — or, when
   the runtime is open, at the length this material naturally wants. Does it fit? If it does not,
   cut something — do not compress everything.
3. **Objectives against concepts.** Is every learning objective served by a core concept, and does
   every core concept serve an objective? An orphan on either side means the scope is wrong, not
   that a line is missing.
4. **The honest gaps.** Is open_questions empty because nothing is unclear, or because it was
   easier? Is there a prerequisite whose absence would strand this audience?
5. **Teaching, not summarising.** Would a viewer learn something from the video this brief
   describes that reading the source's own abstract would not already give them? If not, the brief
   has restated the source rather than decided what to teach.

If a revision needs a claim you have not recorded, call `record_finding` first, then revise.
