# Humanise

Goal: produce writing that reads as if a real person wrote it in one sitting. AI text gets flagged because it is *too* smooth, *too* even, and reaches for the same vocabulary and structures every time. Human writing is bumpier, more specific, and less tidy. Write toward that.

This skill is about writing quality and natural voice, not about faking authorship of work someone didn't do. Don't use it to pass off AI text as human in contexts where that's dishonest (graded academic work, anything requiring an authorship declaration).

## How detectors decide "AI"

Two measurable signals drive most detectors:

- **Perplexity** — how predictable the next word is. AI keeps perplexity low: safe, expected word choices. Humans are less predictable. So: choose the specific, slightly unexpected word over the generic one.
- **Burstiness** — how much sentence length and complexity *varies*. AI writes uniform, similarly-sized sentences. Humans mix a three-word sentence against a thirty-word one. So: vary sentence length deliberately. Follow a long sentence with a short one. Fragment occasionally.

Everything below is downstream of these two ideas plus the vocabulary/formatting habits editors have catalogued.

## Vocabulary to avoid (AI defaults)

These words scream AI when they pile up. Reach for plainer, more specific alternatives:

delve, intricate, tapestry, pivotal, underscore, landscape, foster, testament, enhance, crucial, vital, meticulous, realm, navigate, robust, seamless, leverage, showcase, boasts, elevate, unlock, harness, embark, myriad, plethora, nuanced, holistic, resonate, cutting-edge, game-changer, transformative, groundbreaking, ever-evolving, fast-paced.

Not banned forever — just don't lean on them. One "crucial" is fine; three in a paragraph is a tell.

## Phrases and openers to cut

- Editorial hedges: "It's important to note that," "It's worth mentioning," "Needless to say."
- Fake authority: "Industry experts say," "Studies show," "Some critics argue" — with no actual source.
- Recap filler: "In conclusion," "In summary," "Overall," "At the end of the day" — especially in short pieces that need no recap.
- Customer-service sign-offs bolted onto real writing: "I hope this helps!", "Let me know if you need anything else," "I hope this message finds you well" (unless the channel genuinely calls for it).
- Puffery: "plays a vital role," "serves as a testament to," "stands as a," "a lasting impact," "in today's fast-paced world."

## Structural tics to avoid

- **Negative parallelism**: "It's not just X, it's Y." "This isn't a feature — it's a revolution." Manufactured depth. Kill it.
- **Rule of three**: AI defaults to three items — "convenient, efficient, and innovative." Use two, or four, or one. Break the triplet reflex.
- **False ranges**: "From strategic planning to execution," "from small startups to global enterprises." Sounds comprehensive, says nothing. Name the actual thing instead.
- **Uniform paragraphs**: every paragraph the same 3–4 sentence length. Vary it — some one-liners, some longer.
- **Present-participle significance tails**: "..., highlighting its importance," "..., reflecting the broader trend," "..., emphasizing the shift." Just stop the sentence.

## Punctuation and formatting

- **Em dashes**: AI overuses them. Prefer a comma, a period, or parentheses. Keep at most one em dash per few paragraphs.
- **Bold**: don't bold key terms all over the place. Bold is for genuine emphasis, rarely.
- **"Term: definition" lists**: avoid the reflexive bulleted "**Thing:** explanation" pattern unless a list is truly the clearest form.
- **Lists vs prose**: don't convert everything into numbered/bulleted lists. Write in paragraphs by default; list only when the content is genuinely enumerable.
- **Emoji in headers** (🚀, ✅): drop them unless the channel is casual and the person actually uses them.
- **Curly “smart” quotes and en dashes** in casual text can read as machine-formatted; plain quotes are more natural in chat/email.

## Positive moves (what human writing does)

- **Vary sentence length on purpose.** Short. Then a longer one that carries a couple of clauses and lets the thought breathe. That variance *is* burstiness.
- **Be concrete.** Specific nouns, real numbers, actual examples beat abstract summary. "Cut onboarding from 6 clicks to 2" over "streamlined the onboarding experience."
- **Let some imperfection stand.** A short aside, a contraction, starting a sentence with "And" or "But," a mild opinion. Perfectly balanced prose reads synthetic.
- **Say one thing directly** instead of hedging every claim.
- **Cut the intro and the outro.** Start at the point. End when you're done, no summary.
- **Prefer the plain word.** "use" not "utilise," "help" not "facilitate," "about" not "regarding," "so" not "as such."

## Quick self-check before sending

1. Read it aloud in your head — does any sentence sound like a brochure? Rewrite it.
2. Do sentence lengths vary, or are they all the same size? Add a short one, break a long one.
3. Scan for the vocabulary list and the filler phrases. Cut or swap.
4. Count em dashes and bold spans. Too many? Thin them out.
5. Any triplet, "not X but Y," or "from X to Y"? Reword one.
6. Delete the opener if it's a greeting-for-greeting's-sake and the closing recap if there is one.
